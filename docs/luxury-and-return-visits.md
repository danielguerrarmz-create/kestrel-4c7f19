# Bower: proposed luxury and engagement improvements

15 September 2026

## Executive recommendation

Make Bower feel like a distinctive commission delivered by a capable practice. The site already provides atmosphere, editorial restraint and memorable imagery. Its weakest connection is between imagining the finished landscape and understanding how to begin with confidence.

Prioritise three changes: evidence of real making, a clearer first-step explanation, and a coherent visual story carried from advertising into the site. Add return-visit features only when they help a prospective patron progress.

This is a review of the current source, rendered homepage and public journey. No visitor analytics or patron interviews were analysed. Research supports the mechanisms discussed below; the proposed Bower implementations need validation.

## 1. Turn the material section into evidence

**Observed:** The homepage's “Joint study 01” uses a concept image. Its copy explains singularity, and its five labels name disciplines, but the section offers little inspectable evidence of how this particular object will be made. Practice already contains founders and earlier work; those assets should be connected to the product more directly.

**Proposed change:** Replace the concept joint image with a real prototype photograph when available. Use one full view and one close detail, each with a factual caption: material, process, date and maker. Add one sentence explaining the decision the prototype tests. Link to the relevant process or prior-work detail. Until a prototype exists, use a clearly identified earlier fabrication example, explaining its relevance without presenting it as a built Bower.

**Why:** Fuchs, Schreier and van Osselaer's four studies found a positive handmade effect partly associated with perceived human care. Their findings do not imply that Bower should claim to be handmade. Show the actual contribution of judgment, fitting, fabrication and planting expertise. [The Handmade Effect, 2015](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jm.14.0018).

**Priority:** Highest. Requires genuine source material and verified captions.

## 2. Make the founding invitation easier to understand

**Observed:** “The first three landscapes will decide what a Bower is” promises influence but can also suggest that the purchaser will resolve an unfinished proposition. Making states the paid four-week study; Questions explains it further. The final homepage invitation does not reveal that sequence.

**Proposed homepage copy to test:**

> Three landscapes. Three founding commissions.
>
> Each begins with a conversation about the place and what might happen there.
>
> Discuss your landscape →

On Contact, add a small “What happens next” explanation beside the form: an initial conversation, then a separately agreed site study if there is a fit. Link “What the site study includes” to the existing Questions section. Confirm the wording against the actual commercial process before publication. Keep the existing number only while it describes real capacity.

**Why:** This is a journey-design recommendation, not a psychological finding. It lets the reader distinguish an exploratory enquiry from a paid commitment. Keep fees and scope readily available in Making/Questions without turning the homepage into a rate card.

**Priority:** Highest. Small implementation; needs confirmation of the service sequence.

## 3. Give the imagery an identifiable centre

**Observed:** The hero moves between four concept scenes every five seconds; later sections add several more. Variety proves range, but can leave a first-time visitor unsure which form is the clearest expression of Bower.

**Proposed change:** Choose one lead study for the homepage and campaign. Show its whole form, a human-scale experience and a material detail. Keep the wider variations in Works. Retain the new evolution triptych as its own clearly identified study. Where images depict different structures, label them as different concepts rather than implying photographic continuity.

Use the existing quiet wordmark consistently. Maintain a recognisable arch/lattice silhouette across image selection and advertising. Avoid adding decorative gold, crests or generic luxury claims.

**Research context:** Han, Nunes and Dreze found different preferences for conspicuous and discreet luxury signals across consumer segments. This supports considering recognisable subtlety, not assuming every wealthy person likes minimalism. [USC account of the 2010 research](https://today.usc.edu/when-the-quiet-logo-speaks-volumesmarshall-school-study-finds-less-is-more-in-luxury-brands/).

Keep the flowers' colour. A seven-study colour paper found that muted colours could increase luxury status through heritage associations, but the effect weakened for recently founded brands and reversed under innovative positioning. Bower should not borrow a centuries-old-house aesthetic indiscriminately. The research concerns product colour; applying it to website imagery is an inference. [Zhou et al., published online 2025; journal issue 2026](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/52/6/1232/8120421).

**Priority:** High. Requires art direction and selection, not new site features.

## 4. Make advertising carry one concrete occasion

**Proposed creative:** One image of a gathering at believable scale. Headline: “A place in the garden. A reason to gather.” Landing page: the same study and occasion, followed by growth over time, making evidence and the invitation to discuss a landscape. This is proposed campaign copy, not approved production text.

Develop separate creative only for genuinely different audiences or uses. Preserve image and message continuity from the advertisement or outreach link to the landing page. Avoid opening with an abstract innovation claim and making the visitor infer the experience.

**Why:** Escalas's advertising experiment connects encouraged mental simulation with narrative immersion and favourable evaluations. It supports helping the viewer picture an occasion; it does not establish this headline's conversion performance. [Imagine Yourself in the Product, 2004](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00913367.2004.10639163).

**Priority:** High when outreach or paid campaigns are active.

## 5. Build a useful return path

Start with a concise, ungated commission overview that can be shared with a partner, estate manager or adviser: a lead study, uses, indicative development stages, how the first appointment works and contact details. Give it a stable URL and a visible “Share this study” action in Works.

Then, if prospects actually compare studies, add “Save study”: a shortlist that works without an account, survives a return visit on the same browser and can be shared by URL. Carry the selected study into the enquiry. Explain that the selection expresses a preference rather than reserving a finished design.

Baymard's product-page research observes demand for saving and comparing items and friction from account requirements. This is ecommerce evidence, so its application to bespoke commissions remains a hypothesis. [Baymard product-page research](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux).

Do not justify this feature with the IKEA effect alone: selecting a favourite is not equivalent to successfully constructing a product. Its strongest initial case is practical coordination.

**Priority:** Shareable overview first; shortlist second, subject to observed need.

## 6. Replace empty anticipation with real progress

Publish a studio note only when there is something specific to show: prototype trial, material decision, planting experiment or site milestone. Each note should contain a date, one strong image, what changed and the next question under investigation.

Offer “Receive the next studio note” below the article, with separate consent from project enquiries. Do not promise a monthly publication until the practice can sustain it. Start with the first substantive note before building a subscription workflow.

**Priority:** After evidence gathering. This is an editorial/product hypothesis, not a proven retention intervention.

## Changes to remove or reduce now

- Test eliminating the roughly three-second desktop wordmark introduction. Put the object in the first frame; animate the mark only if it does not delay access.
- Replace automatic hero rotation with a still or deliberate controls. Judge it by comprehension and enquiry progression, not animation preference alone.
- Raise desktop captions from 8–9px to 11–12px and improve contrast. Keep expressive display type, but reduce repeated all-cap labels and competing microcopy.
- Consolidate “Valley at dawn / Morning mist” to one caption. Keep sections that reveal a new fact or experience.
- Keep native, gentle snapping. Do not force tall content into clipped screens or intercept wheel input.

## Delivery order and acceptance

1. **First pass:** caption legibility, opening/hero simplification, founding invitation and the linked first-step explanation. Check keyboard, touch, reduced motion, zoom and narrow landscape layouts.
2. **Next content pass:** prototype evidence, one lead study and a shareable commission overview. Every caption should distinguish concepts, tests and completed work accurately.
3. **Then:** one real studio note; add a shortlist only if patron conversations show a need.

Run five to eight moderated sessions with plausible patrons or advisers. Ask them to explain what Bower is, what is built versus conceptual, what an enquiry leads to and what they would share with another decision-maker. This identifies misunderstandings, not statistically reliable conversion uplift.

Measure qualified enquiries per visitor, progression to Making/Practice, return visits to shared studies and enquiry completion. Define qualification with the practice before counting it. With low traffic, treat before/after changes as directional and use interviews; do not declare an A/B winner from a handful of enquiries.

## Already implemented

Homepage evolution image replacement and responsive WebP assets; complete image proportions; gentle homepage snapping; height-aware desktop composition; flexible mobile layouts and reduced-motion support. The recommendations above are proposals, not additional implemented changes.
